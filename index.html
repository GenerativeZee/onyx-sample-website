The room

No one hands you a menu

Onyx opened in a basement that used to be a car park. We kept the low ceiling and the concrete, then spent the budget on light — every table sits in its own pool of it, and everything past the next table falls away into the dark.

The disc set into each tabletop replaced the menu on our second week. A guest asked what the chicken looked like; the kitchen sent a plate out for him to look at, and the plate came back cold. Now the food shows itself first and arrives hot.

The kitchen is eight people and one wood oven running at 430°C from six until close. Nothing is held. If a dish takes eleven minutes, it takes eleven minutes.
